Key outcome

01 — Problem

The generation that knows about stress, and drowns in it anyway.

Gen Z is the most mental-health-literate generation we've had, and the most anxious. Existing wellness apps ask for attention at exactly the moment there's none to spare — a journal prompt, a five-minute breathwork flow — and unmanaged teenage stress quietly compounds into physical and chronic illness.

I designed for teenagers aged 12–18 in Germany — secondary-school students, social-media natives, with an attention window measured in seconds.

How might we

…help teenagers notice rising stress before it peaks — without asking for attention they don't have?

02 — Research

By the time you feel stressed, your body has been signalling for hours.

I combined interviews with teens and supporters, secondary research into stress physiology — toxic stress, biological embedding, physiological dysregulation — and a stakeholder map that narrowed focus to those directly affected and the people around them.

  1. Sense, don't ask

    Start from the body's signals, not a daily check-in.

    Catch the rise, not the crisis.

04 — Ideate → Design

A ring, a nudge, a pause.

The ring continuously reads the body; when a pattern suggests rising stress, the app surfaces one small action sized for the moment — a 30-second breath, a shift in posture, a message from someone who matters. "kāri," a soft companion character, guides without lecturing.

Biometric-first sensing
WhyReading the body removes the need for a teen to self-report — the intervention can begin before they consciously notice stress.
One action, sized for the moment
WhyA Stress button, Journal, Calendar, and the kāri buddy each offer a single glanceable step — never a queue of tasks.
Stress-mapped colour system
WhyThe interface darkens as stress rises and lifts toward soft lilac as it eases, making an invisible state visible at a glance.

Wellbeing that meets you before you ask.

What I learned

  • Ambient, sensed care earns trust by being light on attention and heavy on timing.
  • For teens, tone is a feature — "knows me" beats "instructs me".
  • Designing the wearable and the app together kept the experience coherent.

If I continued

  • Validate the stress-detection model with longitudinal biometric data.
  • Co-design the kāri character's voice with teens directly.
  • Push the concept further during my master's at TH Augsburg.

My contribution

A solo project — I owned the research, the industrial design of the ring, the service architecture, and the full UI and hi-fi prototype, including the stress-to-interface colour system.
