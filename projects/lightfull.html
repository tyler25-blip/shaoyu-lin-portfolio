05UIInstallation2024

Lightfull Studio

Game-style UI for a touch-driven interactive wall at a cybersecurity exhibition — making an abstract topic playable for walk-up visitors.

Role
Freelance UI Designer
Timeline
2024
Client
Lightfull Studio × ZYXEL
Scope
Game flow · HUD UI
Key outcome

A complete game flow and sci-fi HUD interface for a touch wall seen by hundreds of exhibition visitors, weaving three partner brands into the experience.

01 — The brief

A serious topic, a crowd that's walking past.

At a cybersecurity exhibition, the challenge isn't information — it's attention. Visitors drift between booths with seconds to spare, and "cybersecurity" is abstract and easily ignored. Lightfull Studio needed an interactive wall that stopped people, taught them something, and left the partner brands memorable.

The users were walk-up visitors of every background, standing at a touch wall in a busy hall.

How might we

…turn a cybersecurity booth into a game walk-up visitors actually want to play — and remember the brands behind it?

02 — Audience & constraints

Designing for ten-second attention and a giant touch surface.

I designed for fleeting attention: the interaction had to be legible at a glance, satisfying within seconds, and operable by anyone, on a wall-scale touch surface. Three partner brands — ZYXEL among them — had to be woven into the story without breaking the game.

  1. Playable in one look

    The goal and the next tap are always obvious.

  2. Reward, then inform

    Win the moment first; deliver the message inside the payoff.

  3. Brands as allies, not banners

    Partners appear as in-game solutions, not interruptions.

03 — Design

Defeat the threat, earn the intel.

The flow runs as a game: an intro draws you in, a "ZeroDay" boss threat appears, you fend off attacks, and a results screen scores your performance — then surfaces real security solutions from the partner brands, with a QR code to take away. A sci-fi HUD ties it together.

A boss-battle game loop
WhyFraming threats as a "ZeroDay" boss makes an abstract risk concrete and gives visitors a clear, winnable goal.
A "power assessment" results screen
WhyScoring the player ("qualified agent · 1200 pts") rewards the moment and earns the few seconds needed to deliver the brand message.
Sci-fi HUD system
WhyA consistent heads-up language keeps a multi-screen flow readable at wall scale and on the move.
04 — Outcome

A booth that drew a crowd.

The wall shipped and ran live at the exhibition, drawing hundreds of visitors through the full game-to-solution journey.

What I learned

  • For walk-up interfaces, clarity beats depth every time.
  • A reward earns you the right to deliver a message.
  • Brand goals and player experience can align if framed as the same win.

If I continued

  • Instrument the wall to measure dwell time and drop-off.
  • A/B test the intro hook for stop rate.
  • Add a two-player mode to draw small crowds.

My contribution

As freelance UI designer, I designed the game flow and the full sci-fi HUD system — intro, threat, results, and brand-solution screens — in collaboration with Lightfull Studio and the partner brands.
